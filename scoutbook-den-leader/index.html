Your Den

Logging in and finding your Scouts

Logging In

advancements.scouting.org
  1. Sign in with your my.Scouting username and password — the same account you use for YPT and registration.
  2. Open the Currently viewing pulldown in the top right.
  3. Select your Pack leadership position.
Pick the position you’re working as
TipIf you hold more than one role or belong to more than one unit, that pulldown controls what you see. Wrong menu options? Check it first.
Scoutbook Plus lives at advancements.scouting.org. It uses your my.Scouting credentials, so there's no separate account to create. After login, use the pulldown menu at the top right to select your Pack leadership position. Source: "Introduction to Scoutbook Plus for Cub Scout Packs," Scoutbook Knowledge Base.

Your Home Base: The Roster

Left nav: Roster, Calendar, Reports · Toolbar: Unit Quick Entry, Run Report, Show: Filtered
After you pick your position, you land on the Pack Roster. This is where most den leader work starts. Point out on the screenshot: - Left navigation: Roster, Calendar, Profile, Reports, Activity Logs, Forum. - Tabs above the roster: Roster, Pending Items, History. - Toolbar: Add Individual, Add Den, Unit Quick Entry, Run Report, Show: Filtered. - The Den column. We'll use every one of those in the next few slides.

Finding Your Den on the Roster

Show your dens

  1. Go to the Roster page.
  2. Click the Den column heading to toggle and display existing dens.

Filter to just your den

  1. Click Show: Filtered on the right of the toolbar.
  2. Check your den, then Show Results.
Heads upScouts must be moved into a den before advancement can be recorded. Missing a Scout? Ask your Cubmaster or Pack admin to move them.
Two jobs here: see your dens, then narrow the roster to just your den so you're not scrolling past the whole pack. The filter panel also lets you filter by type (Youths, Adults) and status. Clicking Show Results applies and saves the filter. Big gotcha: advancement can't be recorded until the Scout is placed in a den. This is the number one "why can't I enter anything?" problem at the start of the year when Scouts cross over. Source: "Introduction to Scoutbook Plus for Cub Scout Packs" — Creating Dens and Assigning Scouts; Viewing Adults in your Pack; Editing and Advancing Already Existing Dens.

Advancement

Recording adventures and getting them approved

The Four Advancement Statuses

Started

Work is in progress.

Scout, parent, or leader
›

Completed

Marked done by the Scout or a parent/leader on their behalf. Still needs leader sign-off.

Scout, parent, or leader
›

Approved

A leader verified it. This is the official “earned” status.

Registered leaders only
›

Awarded

Optional. The approved item was physically presented to the Scout.

Leaders · optional
  • Parents who aren’t registered leaders—and Scouts themselves—can mark Completed but cannot mark Approved.
  • Families can enter Completed items from Scoutbook or the Scouting mobile app.
  • Marking an item Awarded removes it from open Purchase Orders — coordinate with whoever buys your pack’s loops and pins.
This is the single most important slide in the deck. Everyone needs a shared vocabulary. Started = in progress. Completed = the Scout or a parent says "we did it." Can be entered on Scoutbook.com or in the Scouting app. It still needs a leader to sign off. Approved = a leader verified it. This is what counts. Awarded = optional bookkeeping that the item was physically handed to the Scout. Parents who aren't registered leaders, and Scouts, can mark Completed but cannot Approve. Awarded caution: marking Awarded removes the item from open Purchase Orders. If your Advancement Chair uses purchase orders to buy awards, don't mark things Awarded before they've placed the order. Talk to them about your pack's process. Source: "Started, Completed, Approved, Awarded in Scoutbook Plus," Scoutbook Knowledge Base.

Checking a Scout’s Progress

  1. On the Roster, click the Scout’s name.
  2. Their profile shows every rank with a progress bar.
  3. Click View More on the current rank to drill in.
The arrow marks View More on the current rank
Clicking a Scout on the roster opens their youth profile with advancement progress for every rank. The current rank's View More button takes you to the rank page. Source: "Introduction to Scoutbook Plus for Cub Scout Packs" — Entering Advancements.

Inside a Rank

The six required adventures on top, electives below, each with its status
Whole den at once?There’s currently no single “den at a glance” progress view. Go Scout by Scout, or run Reports › Advancements › History › Quick Report.
View More shows the required adventures plus the electives, and whether each is started or completed. A very common forum question is "how do I see my whole den's progress on one screen?" The honest answer right now is that there isn't one. Leaders go Scout by Scout or use Reports > Advancements > History > Quick Report. Practical habit: keep your own simple den tracker (paper or spreadsheet) of what you covered each meeting, then use Unit Quick Entry to record it all at once. Sources: "Introduction to Scoutbook Plus for Cub Scout Packs" — Entering Advancements; Scouting Forums threads on viewing advancement for all Scouts in a den.

One Scout: Individual Quick Entry

Best for a Scout who did an adventure at home or made up a missed meeting.

  1. Open the Scout’s adventure page.
  2. Click Quick Entry (top right).
  3. Check requirements under Complete or Approve — Approve auto-checks Complete.
  4. Enter the date and click Save.
TipLeaders can also back-fill previous ranks — handy for transfers or work that was never recorded.
Individual Quick Entry lets you mark several requirements in one go for one Scout instead of clicking each requirement separately. Choosing Approved automatically selects Completed too, so you don't need two passes. Back-filling previous ranks is for record-keeping, which helps with transfers and catching up on missed recording. Source: "Introduction to Scoutbook Plus for Cub Scout Packs" — Individual Quick Entry.

The Whole Den: Unit Quick Entry

Your after-meeting workhorse: record one adventure for every Scout who was there.

  1. From the Roster toolbar, open Unit Quick Entry.
  2. Choose Enter Adventures.
Heads upUnit Quick Entry only works for Scouts who are in the correct den. Missing someone? Check their den assignment.
This is the one to master. Unit Quick Entry is on the roster toolbar. Enter Adventures is the first option in the menu. Again, Scouts have to be in the correct den for them to appear. Source: "Introduction to Scoutbook Plus for Cub Scout Packs" — Unit Quick Entry.

Unit Quick Entry: Record & Approve

  1. Select the Rank, Adventure, and Version, then Apply.
  2. On the left, select the Scouts who did the work.
  3. On the right, select the requirements.
  4. Enter the date, check Mark as Approved (leaders only).
  5. Click Apply.
After each den meeting, take five minutes: pick rank/adventure/version, Apply, check the Scouts who attended, check the requirements you covered, put in the meeting date, mark Approved, Apply. Version matters: adventures get revised, so pick the version your den is actually using. Only leaders can mark Approved. Source: "Introduction to Scoutbook Plus for Cub Scout Packs" — Unit Quick Entry.

Approving What Families Submit

When parents mark work Completed at home, it waits for you.

  1. Click the Pending Items tab above the Roster toolbar.
  2. Review everything marked Completed but not yet Approved.
  3. Click into the Scout to verify and approve.
Make it a habitCheck Pending Items on a regular schedule (say, the night before each den meeting) so families see their Scout’s work acknowledged quickly.
This is your approval inbox. Anything a parent or Scout marked Completed shows up on the Pending Items tab until a leader approves it. The red badges show counts for Ranks, Adventures, and so on. Tell families at the start of the year: "If you do an adventure at home, mark it Completed in the Scouting app and I'll approve it." Source: "Introduction to Scoutbook Plus for Cub Scout Packs" — How to See if an Advancement is Marked Completed But Not Approved.

Calendar & Events

Reminders, RSVPs, and attendance on autopilot

Creating a Den Event

  1. One-time setup: set the unit’s time zone under unit Settings.
  2. Click Calendar in the left nav.
  3. Click a date, then the + icon (or the general + and set the date yourself).
  4. Choose which calendar(s) it appears on — e.g., both the Pack and your Den.
Month view · the blue + in the top right adds an event
Set the time zone once before anyone creates events, or times will be off. It's under the settings gear next to "Currently viewing." Calendar is in the left navigation. Click a date and the plus icon. An event can live on multiple calendars, so a pack hike can sit on the Pack calendar and your Den calendar at the same time. Who can create events and manage attendance depends on your registered or functional position. If you don't see the option, check with your pack's Key 3. Source: "Using Scoutbook Plus Calendar," Scoutbook Knowledge Base.

Event Details That Do the Work

  • Description is required and visible to everyone.
  • Notes / Agenda / Minutes are admin-only — a good home for your meeting plan.
  • Permission slip: optionally require the standard form.
  • Location: add an address and map link.
  • Reminders: up to three, sent automatically.
  • Attendees: add your den, plus outside guests.
Description is public. Notes, Agenda and Minutes are only visible to admins. Three reminders is the big win: Scoutbook sends them for you. In Parent Power I talked about emailing reminders at least three days before every den activity. This automates that. The screenshot shows the lower half of the event form: Repeat Event, Required Permission Slips, Event with RSVP, Location / Google Map URL, and Event Reminders. Source: "Using Scoutbook Plus Calendar," Scoutbook Knowledge Base.

Collecting RSVPs

As the leader

  • Check Event with RSVP on the event.
  • Add attendees, or RSVP won’t work.

Track replies

  • Yes / No; non-responders show as Maybe.
  • Got a reply by text? Use Quick Entry RSVP.

For families

  • Open the event; use the RSVP dropdown.
  • A Scout with a login can RSVP for self and parents.
Your callScoutbook RSVP is optional. Decide with your pack whether to use it, then tell families where to reply.
Using Scoutbook RSVP is optional. It's up to each pack or den. Some dens prefer a group text or sign-up sheet. Whatever you choose, be consistent and tell families up front so they know where to answer. If you do use it, RSVP is opt-in per event. Check the "Event with RSVP" box. The most common reason RSVP "doesn't work" is that no attendees were added. Non-responders show up as Maybe, so the Maybe list is exactly who to chase before you buy supplies. Quick Entry RSVP lets you record the replies you get in a group text or at pickup. The RSVP Report button is on any RSVP-enabled event. Sources: "Using Scoutbook Plus Calendar" — Event RSVP; Scoutbook Knowledge Base article on Scouts RSVPing to events.

After the Event: Attendance

  • After the event, invitees automatically become “attended.”
  • Edit the list like you edited invitees: remove no-shows, add walk-ins.
  • Accurate attendance shows exactly who needs a make-up.
Pair it upClean up attendance, then run Unit Quick Entry for the Scouts who were there. Same five minutes, both records done.
After the event, the invitee list becomes the attended list. Leaders can edit it, just like editing invitees. Quick Entry Attendance makes that fast. Workflow: right after the meeting, fix attendance, then go straight to Unit Quick Entry and approve the requirements for the same Scouts. Source: "Using Scoutbook Plus Calendar" — Attendance.

Messaging

Reaching just your den’s families

Emailing Your Den

  1. Click Messaging in the left menu.
  2. Click into your Den to message only that group, or Select All for everyone.
  3. Write in the WYSIWYG editor: bold, lists, and links work as-is (no BBCode like legacy Scoutbook).
  4. Send.
TextingWorks only for members whose profile has a mobile number, carrier, and the opt-in box checked. Ask families to set it up at the first meeting.
Messaging is in the left menu. Clicking into a specific den limits recipients to that den. Select All goes to everyone. The editor is WYSIWYG now. If you used legacy Scoutbook, you no longer need BBCode. Point out the Youth Protection notice at the bottom of the composer: messages to Scouts should always copy a parent or a second adult. Formatting tip from Parent Power: headings, bullets, most important info first, real links. Avoid walls of text. Texting needs the recipient's mobile number, carrier, and an opt-in checkbox in their profile. Get families to set that up early. Sources: "Introduction to Scoutbook Plus for Cub Scout Packs" — Messaging; "Formatting Messages," Scoutbook Knowledge Base; Scouting Forums.

Putting It Together: A Den Leader’s Rhythm

When In Scoutbook Plus
Start of year Confirm every Scout is in your den; save your roster filter; ask families to set up the Scouting app and text opt-in.
Each month Put the month’s meetings on the Den calendar with reminders (and RSVP, if you use it); send one organized message covering Den and Pack activities.
Before a meeting Check RSVPs and nudge the “Maybes”; clear the Pending Items tab.
After a meeting Fix attendance, then Unit Quick Entry › Approved for what you covered.
Before Pack meeting Hand your Advancement Chair an up-to-date den so awards can be ordered.
This is my suggested routine, built from what worked for my den. It isn't an official Scouting America procedure. The idea: if you spend about five minutes after each meeting, advancement never piles up and you're never scrambling before a Pack meeting. Coordinate with your Advancement Chair on when to mark items Awarded, because of the purchase-order behavior we covered.
