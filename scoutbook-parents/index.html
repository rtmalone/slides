Scoutbook Plus

A Parent’s Guide to Following Your Scout’s Adventure

Presented by Tyler Malone
Assistant Cubmaster, Pack 3116

Welcome, families. This is the parent companion to the Den Leader session. Scoutbook Plus is where your Scout's advancement record lives. Your den leader records what happens at meetings; you can follow along in real time, record work you do at home, and look up every adventure your Scout can earn. Everything here comes from the Scoutbook Knowledge Base ("Scoutbook Plus Tutorial for Parents") and the official adventure pages on scouting.org.

What You Can Do as a Parent

01

Follow Progress

See your Scout’s rank, adventures, and requirements in real time.

02

Mark Completed

Record family and at-home requirements so your den leader can sign them off.

03

Explore Adventures

Look up every required and elective adventure, with requirements and activity ideas.

04

Stay on Schedule

See the den and pack calendar, sync it to your phone, and RSVP if your den uses it.

What you can’t doParents can mark items Completed, but only a registered unit leader can mark them Approved (signed off). That’s by design.
Four things parents do in Scoutbook Plus. And one thing you can't: approve. Per the Knowledge Base, parents, as only parents, do not have rights to mark any advancement as approved. If you're also a registered leader in the unit, you'll see more options, but check your unit's approval policy before using them. Source: "Scoutbook Plus Tutorial for Parents"; "Preventing a Parent from Approving Advancements," Scoutbook Knowledge Base.

Getting In

Logging in and finding your Scout

Logging In

advancements.scouting.org
  1. Sign in with your my.Scouting account — the one you used to register your Scout.
  2. If you have more than one role, use the Currently viewing pulldown (top right) to pick Parent / Guardian.
  3. Click your child’s name to open their Scoutbook page.
Parent / Guardian appears as one of your connections
Don’t see your Scout?Parents should already be connected to their Scouts. If yours is missing, contact your local council registrar (your den leader can help you reach them).
Same website the leaders use: advancements.scouting.org. Log in with the my.Scouting account you used when you registered your Scout. There's no separate signup. If you're also a leader, or have Scouts in more than one unit, the Currently viewing pulldown switches between your roles. Choose your Parent / Guardian connection. If your child isn't there, the council registrar has to connect you. That's not something the den leader can fix in Scoutbook. Source: "Scoutbook Plus Tutorial for Parents" — Logging in for the first time; Viewing/Editing your child's profile.

Following Progress

What the statuses mean and where to look

Who Does What

Started

Work is in progress.

You, your Scout, or a leader
›

Completed

Someone thinks the Scout is done. Not a sign-off yet.

You, your Scout, or a leader
›

Approved

A leader verified it. Officially earned.

Unit leaders only
›

Awarded

Optional. The badge or loop was presented to your Scout.

Unit leaders
  • Completed is like your Scout raising their hand: “I did it!” Approved is the leader’s initials in the handbook.
  • You can enter Completed items on the website or in the Scouting mobile app.
The gold stages are yours. Started and Completed can be entered by a parent, the Scout, or a leader. Approved is the leader's sign-off. The Knowledge Base compares it to the leader initialing the handbook. As far as Scouting America is concerned, Approved is what counts as earned. Awarded is optional bookkeeping the pack uses to track what's been handed out at pack meetings. Source: "Introduction to Scoutbook Plus for Cub Scout Packs" — What the Different Advancement Statuses Mean; "Why Do Parents Have Full Control?"

Inside a Rank

Click View More on a rank: required adventures on top, electives below
  • Each adventure shows whether it’s not started, started, or completed.
  • For example, the Bear rank takes six required adventures plus any two electives.
From your Scout's page, View More on the current rank shows this. The required adventures are across the top, electives underneath, each with its status. Rank requirements vary a bit by rank. The rank pages on scouting.org (coming up) list exactly what each rank needs. Bear, for example, is six required plus any two electives. Sources: "Introduction to Scoutbook Plus for Cub Scout Packs" — Entering Advancements; scouting.org Bear Adventures page.

Marking Completed

Recording what your Scout did at home

Before You Mark Anything

Before marking anything as complete, discuss with your unit leaders to make sure you are aligned with your unit’s advancement practices.

Scoutbook Plus Tutorial for Parents

Your den leader records

  • Requirements done at den meetings and pack events.
  • The Approval for everything.

You record

  • Family requirements.
  • Anything assigned as homework.
  • Make-ups and electives you agreed on with your den leader.
This is the Knowledge Base's own advice, in capital letters on the parent tutorial. Every pack handles this a little differently. In some packs the den leader enters everything; in others parents are expected to record home work. Ask your den leader how ours works. The split on this slide comes from the tutorial: your Scout's den leader records requirements completed; you record completion of family requirements or those assigned as homework. The third bullet on the right (make-ups and electives) is my suggestion, as long as it's agreed with the den leader first. Source: "Scoutbook Plus Tutorial for Parents" — Viewing Advancements and Marking Requirements Complete.

Marking a Requirement Completed

  1. On your Scout’s page, click View More on the rank.
  2. Click the adventure.
  3. Find the requirement and enter the completion date. Add a comment if helpful.
  4. Click Save.
Leader view shown: the Approved checkbox is leaders only
Path: your Scout, View More on the rank, click the adventure. Each requirement has a completion date and an Add Comment button. Comments are a great way to tell the den leader what you did ("did this one on our family hike Saturday"). This screenshot is from a leader account, so it shows the "Mark as Approved/Recorded" checkbox. As a parent you won't be able to approve. Remember to Save. Source: "Introduction to Scoutbook Plus for Cub Scout Packs" — Entering Advancements.

Several at Once: Quick Entry

  1. On the adventure page, click Quick Entry (top right).
  2. Check each finished requirement in the Complete column.
  3. Enter the date and click Save.
Leave Approve aloneThe Approve column is for leaders. Marking Completed sends it to your den leader’s Pending Items list to sign off.
Quick Entry lets you mark several requirements for one adventure in one go. Per the Knowledge Base, parents who aren't leaders can mark items completed, "which just means someone thinks the Scout is done with the advancement, but it is not a sign off." What happens next: your entries show up on the den leader's Pending Items tab (covered in the Den Leader session), and they approve from there. Source: "Introduction to Scoutbook Plus for Cub Scout Packs" — Individual Quick Entry.

Exploring Adventures

Every rank, adventure, and requirement on scouting.org

Find Your Scout’s Rank

scouting.org/programs/cub-scouts/adventures
Pick a rank: Lion through Arrow of Light
Each rank lists required, elective, and special elective adventures
This is public. No login needed. Anyone can browse it. Pick the rank by grade: Lion (K), Tiger (1st), Wolf (2nd), Bear (3rd), Webelos (4th), Arrow of Light (5th). Each rank page lists the required adventures, the elective adventures, and special electives. Special electives (for Bear: Archery, Slingshot, BB Guns) can only be completed at approved events with qualified instructors, so those aren't backyard projects. Source: scouting.org Cub Scout Adventures and Bear Adventures pages.

Inside an Adventure Page

  • Snapshot: what the adventure is about.
  • Safety Moment: what to check before you start.
  • Requirements: the full list, numbered.
  • Activities: choose one activity for each requirement; See Details gives step-by-step instructions.
Bear Habitat: all nine requirements at a glance
TipIn Scoutbook, the resource page link at the top of an adventure takes you straight to its page.
Every adventure has a page like this. Bear Habitat is the example. Snapshot, Safety Moment, the requirements, and then for each requirement a set of activities to choose from. The page says to choose one activity for each requirement. See Details opens the full activity plan with supplies and steps. Inside Scoutbook, each adventure has a "Check out this resource page" line near the top. Clicking it takes you to that adventure's resource page. Good uses for parents: preview what's coming up at den meetings, plan a make-up for a missed meeting, or pick an elective your Scout is excited about. Check with your den leader first. Sources: scouting.org Bear Habitat adventure page; "Introduction to Scoutbook Plus for Cub Scout Packs" — Entering Advancements.

Staying on Schedule

The den calendar, RSVPs, and your phone

The Den & Pack Calendar

  1. Pick your unit in the Currently viewing pulldown (top right).
  2. Click Calendar in the left menu.
  3. Choose which calendars to show: pack and den.

Put it on your phone

Click the link next to the calendar name at the bottom of the calendar page for its iCal URL. Add it to Google, Apple, or Outlook and new events show up automatically.

Unit selector up top · My Calendars at the right
The calendar is the same one leaders use. Switch to your unit at the top right, then Calendar on the left. Subscribing: at the bottom of the calendar page, next to each calendar name, there's a link that gives you an iCal URL. There's a separate one for the pack and each den. Add it to your phone's calendar app and Scoutbook events will flow in automatically. Google Calendar: Other calendars, Add by URL. It can take up to 24 hours for changes to show in Google. iPhone: add a Subscribed Calendar and paste the link. Mac: Calendar > File > New Calendar Subscription. Source: "Scoutbook Plus Tutorial for Parents" — Viewing Troop Information; Linking the Calendar With a Third Party Calendar.

RSVP for Your Scout

RSVP is optional. Your pack or den decides whether to use it, and leaders turn it on event by event. If an event has RSVP turned on:

  1. Make sure you’re set as parent in the top-right pulldown.
  2. Click the event on the calendar.
  3. On the right, expand My Youth Connections with the chevron.
  4. Select your Scout and set the RSVP.
My Youth Connections, circled at right
Why it mattersIf your den uses RSVP and you don’t answer, you show up as Maybe. Your RSVP tells the den leader how many supplies to bring.
RSVP in Scoutbook is optional. Each pack or den chooses whether to use it, and leaders turn it on per event with the "Event with RSVP" box. Some dens collect RSVPs by text, a sign-up sheet, or another app instead, and that's fine. Follow whatever your den leader asks. When RSVP is on for an event, you can answer from the event page. Look for My Youth Connections on the right, expand it, and pick your Scout. Non-responders show as Maybe on the leader's RSVP report, so please answer even if it's a No. This screenshot happens to be from a troop event. Pack and den events look the same. Sources: "Scoutbook Plus Tutorial for Parents" — RSVPing to an Event as a Parent; "Using Scoutbook Plus Calendar" — Event RSVP.

Your Scout’s Own Account & the App

A login for your Scout

  • Scouts with their own my.Scouting account can review their advancement and see the unit calendar.
  • Get your Scout’s Member ID from your den leader, then create the account at my.Scouting.org.
  • Never share your login with your Scout. Every user needs their own account.

The Scouting app

  • Parents and Scouts can use the Scouting mobile app for advancement and the unit calendar instead of the website.
  • You can mark items Completed from the app too.
Optional, but nice as Scouts get older: they can log in and see their own progress. You no longer need to "invite" a Scout. They just need their own my.Scouting account, created with their Member ID. Never hand a Scout your own login. Every my.Scouting user needs a unique account. The Scouting mobile app is the phone-friendly option for parents and Scouts. The Knowledge Base notes that Completed items can be entered from the app as well as the website. Sources: "Scoutbook Plus Tutorial for Parents" — How to make a my.Scouting.org Account; notice at the top of the tutorial; "Introduction to Scoutbook Plus for Cub Scout Packs" — advancement statuses.

Parent Cheat Sheet

Do

  • Ask your den leader how the pack handles home advancement.
  • Mark family and homework requirements Completed, with a date.
  • Add a comment saying what your Scout did.
  • Browse adventures on scouting.org to preview what’s coming.
  • Subscribe to the den calendar, and RSVP if your den uses it.

Don’t

  • Change your Scout’s name or Member ID.
  • Expect Completed to mean earned. It still needs leader approval.
  • Plan special electives (archery, BB guns, slingshots) at home. Those need approved events.
  • Share your login with your Scout.
